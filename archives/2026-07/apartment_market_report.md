## 2. National market snapshot
[↑ Back to Index](#index)

- **Multifamily rebalancing (mid-2026):** Demand has begun to outpace new supply on a trailing basis in major brokerage reads; national vacancy drifted lower in Q2 2026 while deliveries fell sharply from 2024 peaks ([Cushman & Wakefield Q2 2026 Multifamily MarketBeat](https://www.cushmanwakefield.com/en/united-states/news/2026/07/us-multifamily-marketbeat)).
- **Occupancy:** National occupancy remained resilient near about **95%** in early-2026 capital-markets reads, with wide metro dispersion ([Colliers U.S. Multifamily Capital Markets 2026 Q1](https://www.colliers.com/en/research/nrep-uscm-usmf-colliers-capital-markets-multifamily-report-2026-q1)).
- **Concessions:** Still a primary underwriting risk in oversupplied Sun Belt Class A corridors (months free / fee waivers); Midwest Class B/C generally lighter but not zero.
- **Cap rates:** Transactional / survey multifamily caps clustered about **5.0%–5.8%** nationally by class and deal size in 2026 lender/brokerage screens — verify live prints ([apartment lender cap-rate compilations](https://apartmentloanstore.com/glossary/cap-rate); Q1 2026 capital-markets notes around mid-5%s).
- **Pipeline:** Units under construction as a share of inventory fell toward multi-year lows in mid-2026 brokerage summaries — supportive for occupancy into 2027 if starts stay muted.
- **Jobs / demand context (shared data):** State unemployment (BLS LAUS, June 2026) spans about **2.0%–6.0%**; industries and incomes in §4c–4d.
- **Financing screen:** Agency / bank multifamily and DSCR-style debt still price off higher-for-longer rates; model **DSCR** and interest-rate stress, not only LTV. Exact coupons are lender- and sponsor-specific — treat public “about 5.5%–7%+” chatter as `verify live`.
- **PM screen:** Third-party multifamily often about **3–8% of EGI** or flat $/door; this report defaults about **5–6% of EGI** unless quoted — see §9.
- **Sibling residential context:** Redfin/FHFA house prices in the SFR report are **not** apartment comps; cited only as broad housing-cost context for renter vs owner affordability.

### Yield definition used here
[↑ Back to Index](#index)

- Prefer **cap rate** = NOI ÷ price (label trailing vs forward).
- **Gross yield** = annual rent ÷ price is a crude screen only.
- Prefer **economic occupancy** and **in-place rent after concessions**; label **asking** when that is all you have.
- Cash-flow scores haircut vacancy, concessions, tax, insurance, turnover, and PM about **5–6% of EGI**.

### Core conclusion
[↑ Back to Index](#index)

Best risk-adjusted **apartment** screens in this cut still favor **Midwest metros with deep 5+ unit inventory, workable landlord law, and lighter concession regimes** (Ohio, Indiana, Missouri, Alabama, Tennessee income product), plus selected Southeast markets where **supply visibility** is clear. Sun Belt scale (Texas, Georgia, Carolinas, Arizona, Florida) remains important for liquidity — but **near-term Cash scores** are haircut for concessions, insurance, and delivery hangover. Coastal and heavily regulated markets (California, Oregon, Washington, New York City, D.C., much of New Jersey) stay **specialist-only** for conventional apartment cash-flow buyers.

**Class A vs Class B/C:**

- **Class B/C (especially Midwest garden)** usually wins this screen on going-in yield and lower amenity competition — if you can staff ops and underwrite collections.
- **Class A** can win on liquidity, newer systems, and long-term rent growth **after** lease-up and concession burn-off — do not buy the offering-memorandum asking rent.

## 7. Legal environment — verified 2026 highlights
[↑ Back to Index](#index)

Apartment portfolios are more exposed to **rent caps, just-cause eviction, registration, and transfer/TOPA** rules than scattered SFR. Highlights below are screening notes — confirm with counsel.

| Topic | Apartment relevance |
| ----- | ------------------- |
| **Rent-control preemption states** (many Southern / Midwestern) | Supports remote / scaled apartment ops |
| **California** statewide cap (5% + inflation, max 10%) + local overlays | Binds renewals on covered units |
| **Oregon** statewide cap (about **9.5% for 2026** — verify) + just cause | Caps upside; underwrite renewals |
| **Washington** statewide cap (about **9.683% for 2026** — verify) + just cause | Same |
| **New York** Good Cause / rent stabilization | NYC and opt-in Upstate cities — specialist rent rolls |
| **New Jersey** Anti-Eviction Act + local rent ordinances | Local ordinance check is mandatory |
| **Washington, D.C.** TOPA + rent stabilization | Transfer friction can dominate deals |
| **Chicago / Cook County** | No statewide rent cap (IL preemption) but heavy ordinance / notice rules |
| **Minnesota / Maryland / Connecticut / Maine / Vermont** | Local or statewide tenant tilt — underwrite timelines |

Always separate **state baseline** from **city/county** overlays.

## 8. Insurance and property tax overlays
[↑ Back to Index](#index)

- **Effective property-tax rates** still span roughly about **0.27% (Hawaii)** to **2.23% (New Jersey)** in residential compilations — multifamily assessments can differ; pull the parcel card.
- **Catastrophe insurance** (wind, hail, flood, wildfire) is a first-class NOI line for apartments in Florida, Louisiana, coastal Texas, Oklahoma, Mississippi, coastal Carolinas, and parts of California.
- Model **insurance at quote**, not a national average, before locking Cash scores on Gulf / coastal deals.
- Higher-tax Northeast states need larger OpEx haircuts even when rents look strong.

## 11. Methodology and sources
[↑ Back to Index](#index)

### Scoring method
[↑ Back to Index](#index)

- Pillars scored 1–10; **Econ** = average of Jobs, Price, Cash, Appreciation.
- Rankings are **apartment-specific** judgment using shared jobs/demo/income data plus mid-2026 multifamily market research (occupancy, concessions, deliveries, cap-rate bands).
- **Price** favors easier apartment entry and inventory depth — not Redfin house medians.
- **Cash** haircuts concessions, insurance, taxes, and PM about **5–6% of EGI**.
- Owner/Tenant law aligned with the sibling report but **weighted harder** for rent regulation on apartments.
- Thin apartment states lose rank even when SFR yields look strong in the sibling report.

### Primary sources
[↑ Back to Index](#index)

- Shared pipeline `data/` — BLS LAUS / CES, Census ACS demographics, FRED/CPS income, BEA (context)
- [Cushman & Wakefield — U.S. apartment market Q2 2026](https://www.cushmanwakefield.com/en/united-states/news/2026/07/us-multifamily-marketbeat)
- [Colliers — U.S. Multifamily Capital Markets 2026 Q1](https://www.colliers.com/en/research/nrep-uscm-usmf-colliers-capital-markets-multifamily-report-2026-q1)

- Sibling legal and insurance notes in [`rental_market_report.md`](rental_market_report.md) §§7–8
- Spec: [`apartment_market_spec.md`](apartment_market_spec.md)

### Caveats / data gaps
[↑ Back to Index](#index)

- Many **$/door**, metro **cap rates**, and **economic occupancy** prints are not free/public — marked directional or `unavailable` rather than invented.
- National brokerage vacancy/occupancy definitions differ (e.g. about 5% vacant vs about 95% occupied) — compare like-with-like.
- Illustrative 4e deal sizes are **screens**, not appraisals.
- Asking rents ≠ achieved rents; concessions can move NOI hundreds of bps.
- Demographics are context only.
